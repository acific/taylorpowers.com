SuperFile × Trackhouse NASCAR

SuperFile’s market entry on NASCAR’s biggest stage: a multi-year Trackhouse Racing partnership that started with Shane van Gisbergen’s No. 97 Chevrolet at the 2026 Daytona 500.

Year
2026
Type
Sports Partnership

As VP of Brand at SuperFile, Taylor led the brand-side strategy and activation of a multi-year partnership with Trackhouse Racing, establishing NASCAR as a key platform for introducing an emerging technology company to a national audience.

The partnership launched at the 2026 Daytona 500 with Shane van Gisbergen’s No. 97 Chevrolet and expanded into a multi-race program that brought together national broadcast exposure, creative brand integrations, executive hospitality and business development.

Taylor’s role.

Taylor oversaw the brand-side partnership activation, connecting sponsorship rights to broader marketing and business objectives: strategic planning, brand integration, creative and production oversight, cross-functional coordination, relationship development, merchandising and swag, and VIP hospitality.

The program included the Daytona 500 debut, later race activations, branded content and experiences designed to make the partnership commercially valuable beyond race-day exposure.

VP of Brand, SuperFile.

Sponsorship · Livery · Race-weekend activation · VIP hospitality

The strategy.

The partnership was built to support SuperFile’s market entry, with three objectives: accelerate national brand awareness, establish credibility, and create valuable B2B relationships with prospective customers, partners and industry leaders.

Trackhouse was a strategic fit for its unconventional approach to motorsport, its emphasis on innovation and its willingness to challenge traditional sponsorship models. That shared outlook let the partnership combine NASCAR’s national reach with creative flexibility, executive access and commercial opportunities well beyond race day.

Daytona.

The black and neon-yellow No. 97 started 13th in the Daytona 500 and led laps 101 to 103 before it was caught in the late Stage 2 crash. After repairs it finished 30th, one lap down.

The race set the look for SuperFile’s season: a dark car with the SuperFile mark across the hood.

A season of liveries.

The deal grew into a multi-year, multi-race partnership, and the No. 97 changed its look through the year: a Mr. Brainwash art car for Las Vegas in March, a “SuperFile Salutes” digital-camo military tribute for the Coca-Cola 600 on Memorial Day weekend, and a Chicago-inspired livery by the artist Lefty Out There at Chicagoland in July.

SuperFile was also announced as primary sponsor of Connor Zilisch’s No. 88 for New Hampshire in August, and several of the cars became official Lionel die-casts.

Watkins Glen.

On May 10, van Gisbergen won at Watkins Glen from pole, leading 74 of 100 laps and finishing more than seven seconds clear for his seventh Cup Series victory.

“Yeah, this SuperFile Chevy was great,” he said afterwards.

On track.

Across the season the No. 97 carried SuperFile from Daytona to Martinsville, Texas and beyond, with the crew, the car and the livery all part of the brand.

Impact.

The partnership established SuperFile’s presence in NASCAR, generated national brand visibility and opened the door to executive networking, strategic introductions and early-stage business development.

What began with a Daytona 500 debut became a multi-year racing partnership, and a model for how sports sponsorship can support brand positioning, market entry and commercial relationships.
